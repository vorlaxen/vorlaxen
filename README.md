# Hakan K.

I am the founder and maintainer of [Vorlaxen Labs](https://www.vorlaxen.com). I am a full-stack developer, and most of my time goes to backend systems and DevOps. That covers the service itself, the way we ship it, and the work of keeping it healthy after the first release. I live in Türkiye.

Vorlaxen Labs starts from curiosity, but curiosity is only the start. We turn what fascinates us into things people use. An idea stays with us when it can become a tool: something a person can open, understand, and keep. A plan that sounds good and never leaves the notebook is not the work we want to do.

## The problem comes first

We start by making the problem clear. Before we write much code, we want to know what is going wrong and who has to live with it. The first explanation is often a guess. We test that guess. If the test shows that we misunderstood the need, we say so and we start again.

This part is slow, and it should be slow. A clear problem saves months that we would otherwise spend on the wrong product. We would rather solve a smaller problem that is real than defend a large story that falls apart in the first week of real use.

We also stay close to the people who feel the problem. A need that we only discuss inside the lab is still an assumption. When we can point to a person, a repeated task, or a failure that keeps coming back, we know we are close enough to build.

## We stay until it works

Telling the story of the product is not enough. The first version always misses something. A step that is hard to follow. An error we did not expect. A deploy that works once and then becomes a manual habit. We look for those gaps, we revise, and we stay until the tool is reliable.

Reliable means a normal day, not a demo. Someone should be able to use the tool without us sitting next to them, and they should be able to trust the result. If they have to message us for every unusual case, the product is not ready yet.

This is why backend systems and DevOps are central in my work. A feature that only works on a developer's machine is still a draft. Deployment, logs, and recovery belong to the product. When something fails at night, we need to see what happened and get the service back without guessing. If that part is weak, the product is weak, even when the interface looks finished.

We also care about the next person who will change the system. That person might be a teammate, or it might be us six months later. We want each important decision to be explainable. Some problems are complicated, and the system will be complicated too. Each part should still have a reason that another developer can read and follow.

## If it is not used, it is not done

We judge the work by use. People leave a product when they cannot understand it, when they do not trust it, or when it creates more work than it removes. Clean code does not fix that. A good demo does not fix that either. A product people cannot understand, use, or trust is not finished.

A good tool cuts the noise. It leaves room to think, to make, and to decide. The person using it should spend their attention on their own work, not on fighting the tool. That is the standard at Vorlaxen Labs.

So the path is simple to describe and hard to follow. We find a real need. We test our understanding of it. We build, we revise, and we keep the parts that work in someone's hands. Then we turn those parts into a product people can actually keep using.
